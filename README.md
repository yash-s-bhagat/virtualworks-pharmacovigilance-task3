## 1. Case Study

### Patient Information

- Age: 45 years
- Gender: Female

### Suspected Drug

The case identifies a suspected drug; the drug name is assessed based on the case scenario provided in the training material.

### Adverse Reaction

- Skin rash

### Time to Onset

- The skin rash developed **3 days after starting the drug**.

### Outcome

- The patient **recovered after stopping the drug**.

---

# 2. Causality Assessment

The relationship between the suspected drug and the adverse reaction was assessed using the following criteria:

## A. Temporal Relationship

The adverse reaction occurred **3 days after initiation of the suspected drug**.

This provides a reasonable temporal relationship between drug exposure and the onset of the skin rash.

**Assessment: Positive**

---

## B. Dechallenge

The patient recovered after the suspected drug was stopped.

Improvement following withdrawal of the suspected medicine supports a possible causal relationship.

**Assessment: Positive**

---

## C. Rechallenge

There is no information indicating that the patient was re-exposed to the suspected drug and developed the same reaction again.

**Assessment: Unknown / Not reported**

---

## D. Alternative Causes

No alternative cause is identified in the case information provided.

However, alternative causes should always be considered during pharmacovigilance causality assessment.

**Assessment: No clear alternative cause reported**

---

# 3. Causality Classification

### Final Classification: PROBABLE

The case is classified as **Probable** because:

1. There is a reasonable temporal relationship between drug administration and the reaction.
2. The reaction occurred 3 days after starting the drug.
3. The patient recovered after withdrawal of the suspected drug (positive dechallenge).
4. No clear alternative cause is provided.
5. Rechallenge information is not available.

The available evidence therefore supports a probable relationship between the suspected drug and the skin rash.

---

# 4. Assessment Summary

| Assessment Criterion | Finding |
|---|---|
| Temporal relationship | Positive |
| Onset | 3 days after starting drug |
| Dechallenge | Positive |
| Rechallenge | Unknown / Not reported |
| Alternative causes | No clear alternative cause reported |
| Outcome | Recovered after stopping drug |
| Final causality | **PROBABLE** |

---

# 5. Conclusion

Based on the available case information, the relationship between the suspected drug and skin rash is classified as **PROBABLE**.

The short time interval between drug initiation and reaction, together with recovery after withdrawal of the suspected drug, provides supporting evidence for a causal relationship.

This task helped me understand the importance of temporal relationship, dechallenge, rechallenge and alternative causes in pharmacovigilance causality assessment.

---

## Key Learning

Causality assessment helps evaluate how likely it is that a suspected medicine caused an adverse reaction. In individual case assessment, the conclusion is based on the clinical history and available evidence and can be revised if additional information becomes available.
